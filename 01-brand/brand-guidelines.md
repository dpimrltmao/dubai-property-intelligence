# Dubai Property Intelligence
## Brand Guidelines — Version 1.0

**Brand:** Dubai Property Intelligence  
**Publisher:** MRLTMAO  
**Publication:** Daily Intelligence Report  
**Market:** Dubai, United Arab Emirates

---

## 1. Purpose

Dubai Property Intelligence (DPI) is a professional daily real estate intelligence publication designed to help property advisors, investors, buyers, sellers, landlords, developers and business professionals understand the Dubai property market.

DPI does not simply report news.

Every important piece of information should answer:

> What does this mean for Dubai real estate?

The goal is to turn complex property, economic and geopolitical information into clear, useful and actionable intelligence.

---

DPI should feel:

- Professional
- Intelligent
- Independent
- Trustworthy
- Data-driven
- Premium
- Clear
- Modern
- Practical

- Like a property advertisement
- Like a sales brochure
- Sensationalist
- Clickbait
- Politically biased
- Overly complicated
- Full of unnecessary jargon

## 3. Brand Voice

DPI should communicate like a senior real estate analyst speaking to an intelligent investor.

The writing should be:

- Clear
- Direct
- Calm
- Evidence-based
- Confident but not arrogant
- Analytical
- Easy to understand

Complex economic concepts should be explained in simple language.

---

## 4. Core Question

Every report should continually answer:

> "What does this mean for Dubai property?"

News should never be included simply because it is interesting.

It should be included because it could affect:

- Property prices
- Rental prices
- Transaction activity
- Investor demand
- Foreign investment
- Mortgage affordability
- Developer activity
- Construction
- Tourism
- Business activity
- Employment
- Liquidity
- Market confidence

---

## 5. Editorial Principles

### Accuracy First

Never invent statistics, quotations, transactions, prices, forecasts or sources.

If reliable information cannot be confirmed, clearly state that the information is unavailable or uncertain.

### Source First

Important statistics should be supported by reliable sources.

Priority should generally be given to:

1. Government and official sources
2. Regulatory bodies
3. Central banks
4. Recognized financial institutions
5. Established financial journalism
6. Major real estate consultancies
7. Reputable research organizations

### Facts vs Analysis

Clearly distinguish between:

**FACT**

What the available data or source says.

**ANALYSIS**

What DPI believes the information could mean.

**FORECAST**

What could happen in the future based on available evidence.

Forecasts must never be presented as guaranteed outcomes.

---

## 6. Rumor vs Reality

Every report may include a section called:

# Rumor vs Reality

The purpose is to examine common claims about Dubai real estate.

The format should be:

### Rumor

State the claim clearly.

### Reality

Explain what the available evidence actually shows.

### Advisor Takeaway

Explain what buyers, sellers or investors should understand.

DPI should never present an unverified rumor as fact.

---

## 7. Market Radar

Every report should identify:

### Top Opportunities

Potential developments, communities, asset classes, market conditions or trends that may create opportunities.

### Top Risks

Potential developments, communities, asset classes, economic conditions or geopolitical events that could negatively affect the market.

Opportunities and risks should be supported by evidence whenever possible.

---

## 8. Confidence Score

DPI may assign a confidence level to major conclusions.

### HIGH

Strong evidence from multiple reliable sources.

### MEDIUM

Reasonable evidence exists, but uncertainty remains.

### LOW

Limited evidence, conflicting information or significant uncertainty.

Confidence measures the strength of the evidence.

It does NOT mean that the outcome is guaranteed.

---

## 9. MRLTMAO Index

The MRLTMAO Index is DPI's proprietary market sentiment indicator.

The index will eventually score the Dubai property market from:

# 0–100

The index should combine measurable market indicators with clearly defined methodology.

The initial proposed categories are:

- Transactions — 20%
- Prices — 20%
- Luxury Market — 15%
- Rentals — 10%
- Mortgage Conditions — 10%
- Foreign Investment — 10%
- Global Economy — 10%
- Geopolitical Risk — 5%

The methodology may be refined as better data becomes available.

Any change to the methodology should be documented.

The index should never be manipulated to create a desired narrative.

---

## 10. Market Score Interpretation

### 85–100
Very Strong / Bullish

### 70–84
Strong / Positive

### 55–69
Healthy / Neutral-Positive

### 40–54
Cautious

### 0–39
Defensive / Weak

The score should always be accompanied by an explanation of the major factors driving it.

## 14. World News Impact Framework

For important global events, DPI should explain:

### 1. What happened?

The factual event.

### 2. Why does it matter?

The economic or geopolitical significance.

### 3. How could it affect the UAE?

Potential impact on the UAE economy.

### 4. How could it affect Dubai property?

Potential effect on:

- Buyers
- Sellers
- Investors
- Developers
- Rentals
- Prices
- Liquidity

### 5. What should an advisor watch?

The next indicator or event that could confirm or contradict the analysis.

---

## 15. Client Talking Point

Every report should provide at least one concise insight that a property advisor can use with a client.

It should sound natural when spoken or sent through WhatsApp.

It should educate rather than pressure the client into buying.

---

## 16. Social Media

Every daily report may produce adapted content for:

- LinkedIn
- Instagram
- Facebook
- WhatsApp

Social media content should remain factual and should not exaggerate market conditions.

---

## 17. Editorial Independence

DPI should not promote a developer, project, community or investment simply because it is commercially convenient.

If a project is discussed positively, the reasoning should be clear.

Risks and disadvantages should also be acknowledged.

---

## 18. Daily Report Standard

Before publication, every report should be checked for:

- Accuracy
- Source quality
- Date accuracy
- Numerical accuracy
- Clear distinction between fact and opinion
- Logical analysis
- Balanced presentation
- Professional language
- Spelling and grammar
- Consistent branding

---

## 19. The Golden Rule

# Do not tell people what to think.

# Give them the information they need to think better.
