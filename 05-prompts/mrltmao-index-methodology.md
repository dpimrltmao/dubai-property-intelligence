# MRLTMAO Index
## Methodology — Version 1.0

**Publication:** Dubai Property Intelligence  
**Index:** MRLTMAO Index  
**Publisher:** MRLTMAO  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Scale:** 0–100

---

## 1. Purpose

The MRLTMAO Index is a daily indicator designed to summarize the health, momentum and risk environment of the Dubai real estate market.

The index is intended to help property advisors, investors, buyers, sellers and market observers understand the overall direction of the market.

The index is an analytical tool.

It is NOT financial advice and does not guarantee future property performance.

---

# 2. Index Structure

The index contains eight components.

| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Property Transactions | 20% |
| Property Prices | 20% |
| Luxury Market | 15% |
| Rental Market | 10% |
| Mortgage Conditions | 10% |
| Foreign Investment | 10% |
| Global Economy | 10% |
| Geopolitical Risk | 5% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

Each component receives a score between 0 and 100.

The final index is the weighted average of all component scores.

---

# 3. Basic Calculation

The calculation is:

Index Score =

(Transaction Score × 20%)

+

(Price Score × 20%)

+

(Luxury Score × 15%)

+

(Rental Score × 10%)

+

(Mortgage Score × 10%)

+

(Foreign Investment Score × 10%)

+

(Global Economy Score × 10%)

+

(Geopolitical Risk Score × 5%)

---

# 4. Market Score Interpretation

| Score | Classification |
|---:|---|
| 85–100 | Very Strong / Bullish |
| 70–84 | Strong / Positive |
| 55–69 | Healthy / Neutral-Positive |
| 40–54 | Cautious |
| 0–39 | Defensive / Weak |

The classification must always be accompanied by an explanation.

---

# 5. Component 1 — Property Transactions

Weight: 20%

This component measures the level and direction of transaction activity in Dubai.

Potential data inputs include:

- Dubai Land Department transaction volume
- Total transaction value
- Residential transactions
- Ready-property transactions
- Off-plan transactions
- Month-on-month changes
- Year-on-year changes

### General scoring principle

Higher transaction activity combined with healthy market fundamentals generally produces a higher score.

Falling transaction activity generally reduces the score.

However, unusually high transaction activity must not automatically be considered positive if it is accompanied by evidence of speculative or distressed activity.

---

# 6. Component 2 — Property Prices

Weight: 20%

This component measures the direction and health of Dubai residential prices.

Potential inputs include:

- Official or reputable residential price indices
- Apartment prices
- Villa prices
- Month-on-month movement
- Year-on-year movement
- Price per square foot
- Segment-specific performance

Price increases are not automatically positive.

Rapid price increases accompanied by affordability deterioration or speculative behavior may reduce the score.

Similarly, moderate price declines may not necessarily be negative if they represent healthy market normalization.

---

# 7. Component 3 — Luxury Market

Weight: 15%

This component measures the health of Dubai's prime and luxury property market.

Potential inputs include:

- AED 5M+ transactions
- AED 10M+ transactions
- AED 15M+ transactions
- Prime transaction volumes
- Luxury transaction values
- Prime rental demand
- International high-net-worth activity

Luxury demand should be analyzed separately from the wider residential market because prime property can behave differently from mass-market housing.

---

# 8. Component 4 — Rental Market

Weight: 10%

This component measures rental-market strength.

Potential inputs include:

- Rental price movement
- Rental transaction volume
- Occupancy
- Rental yields
- New rental supply
- Tenant demand
- Residential vacancy where reliable data exists

Strong rental demand and sustainable rental growth generally increase the score.

Rapid rental increases that significantly reduce affordability should be treated carefully rather than automatically considered positive.

---

# 9. Component 5 — Mortgage Conditions

Weight: 10%

This component measures the financing environment for property buyers.

Potential inputs include:

- UAE Central Bank policy
- US Federal Reserve policy
- Mortgage rates
- Fixed-rate mortgage pricing
- Variable-rate mortgage pricing
- Borrowing costs
- Credit availability

Lower and improving borrowing costs generally support the score.

Higher borrowing costs generally reduce the score.

Because the UAE dirham is pegged to the US dollar, US monetary policy is an important consideration.

---

# 10. Component 6 — Foreign Investment

Weight: 10%

This component measures international investor participation and confidence in Dubai.

Potential inputs include:

- Foreign buyer activity
- International transaction volumes
- Foreign direct investment
- International property exhibitions
- Visa and residency developments
- Population growth
- Tourism growth
- Business formation
- International capital flows

Foreign investment should be evaluated using reliable evidence rather than anecdotal claims.

---

# 11. Component 7 — Global Economy

Weight: 10%

This component measures external economic conditions that could influence Dubai.

Potential inputs include:

- Global GDP growth
- US economic conditions
- European economic conditions
- Chinese economic conditions
- Indian economic conditions
- Inflation
- Interest rates
- Global financial-market conditions
- Oil prices
- Global investor sentiment

The analysis should consider both positive and negative effects.

For example:

Higher oil prices may support Gulf liquidity but may also increase global inflation if driven by supply disruption.

---

# 12. Component 8 — Geopolitical Risk

Weight: 5%

This component measures geopolitical conditions that could affect Dubai's investment environment.

Potential inputs include:

- Regional conflicts
- Shipping disruption
- Strait of Hormuz developments
- International sanctions
- Regional political instability
- Major global conflicts
- Travel disruption

Higher geopolitical stability generally produces a higher score.

Higher geopolitical risk generally reduces the score.

The index should not exaggerate short-term headlines.

The potential economic transmission mechanism must be considered.

---

# 13. Data Quality

Every component should use the best available data.

Preferred hierarchy:

1. Government sources
2. Regulatory bodies
3. Central banks
4. Official statistics
5. Established financial institutions
6. Major real estate consultancies
7. High-quality financial journalism
8. Reputable research organizations

Social media should not be treated as a primary statistical source.

---

# 14. Data Recency

The latest reliable information should be used.

If a component has not been updated recently, the report should clearly state the data date.

The system must never imply that an old figure is today's figure.

---

# 15. Missing Data

If reliable data is unavailable:

- Do not invent a number.
- Do not estimate without clearly labeling the estimate.
- Use the most recent reliable figure when appropriate.
- Clearly state the data date.
- Reduce confidence in the conclusion if necessary.

---

# 16. Confidence Score

Each major index conclusion may receive a confidence rating.

### HIGH

Multiple reliable and recent data sources support the conclusion.

### MEDIUM

Evidence is reasonable but incomplete or mixed.

### LOW

Evidence is limited, old, conflicting or highly uncertain.

Confidence describes the strength of the evidence.

It does NOT describe the probability of a future return.

---

# 17. Market Momentum

The MRLTMAO Index should eventually include a momentum indicator.

Momentum measures whether the market score is improving or deteriorating.

Example:

Current Index: 78

Previous Index: 72

Momentum: +6

This should be displayed as:

**Momentum +6 🟢**

If:

Current Index: 68

Previous Index: 74

Momentum: -6

Display:

**Momentum -6 🔴**

Momentum should never be interpreted without considering the reason for the change.

---

# 18. Daily Change

The report may display:

**MRLTMAO Index: 78/100**

**Daily Change: +3**

**Momentum: Positive**

The report should explain the main reasons for the movement.

---

# 19. Market Radar

The MRLTMAO Index should feed into the daily Market Radar.

The Market Radar should identify:

### Top Opportunities

The five most important potential opportunities.

### Top Risks

The five most important potential risks.

These should be based on evidence available at the time of publication.

---

# 20. Rumor vs Reality

The MRLTMAO Index should not react to unverified rumors.

If a major rumor is circulating, DPI may discuss it separately under:

# Rumor vs Reality

The rumor must be clearly identified as unverified unless reliable evidence confirms it.

---

# 21. No Manipulation Rule

The index must never be changed to achieve a preferred narrative.

If the data produces a negative score, the report must present the negative score.

If the data produces a positive score, the report must present the positive score.

The purpose of the index is measurement, not marketing.

---

# 22. Methodology Changes

Any change to:

- Component weights
- Data sources
- Scoring rules
- Classification thresholds
- Calculation methodology

must be documented as a new methodology version.

Example:

Version 1.0
Initial methodology

Version 1.1
Minor adjustment

Version 2.0
Major methodology revision

Historical index values should not be silently changed.
