## The 60-second intelligence brief

Dubai's property market is becoming more selective. There are fewer transactions than in its busiest periods, and several measures of residential prices have softened. But **a slower market is not proof that every apartment or villa is suddenly good value**.

Savills counted approximately **34,500 residential transactions in Q3 2026**, down **4% quarter-on-quarter**, worth **AED 70.8 billion**. Savills also reported that like-for-like values were relatively stable despite headline price softness. [S1]

Meanwhile, ValuStrat's **September 2026 VPI** recorded residential values **4.5% lower year-on-year** and **0.1% lower month-on-month**. The decline was greater for apartments (**−6.4% year-on-year**) than villas (**−3.4%**). These are changes in a valuation index, not discounts achieved on an individual property. [S2]

**DPI takeaway:** A genuine opportunity is not a red sticker marked “discount”. It is a property bought at a defensible price, with realistic income, manageable risks, and an exit strategy.

**Confidence:** High that the providers reported these figures; moderate for any claim about an individual's negotiating power. DPI has not independently reconstructed provider indices from transaction-level records.

## Editor's letter — What does a buyer actually gain?

Imagine walking into a shop and seeing a sign: **“NOW 5% OFF.”**

Would you buy the product immediately?

Not before asking two questions: *Was the old price fair?* And *is the product any good?*

Property deserves an even stricter test. A home advertised below its former asking price may still be more expensive than similar homes that actually sold. A rental apartment may look profitable until service charges, vacancies and mortgage payments are counted. And a brand-new development may offer a generous payment plan without offering a good exit price.

In Issue 003, we documented an uneven slowdown. **Issue 004 is about the buyer's decision**: how to tell the difference between an opportunity and a convincing advertisement.

### Three different meanings of “cheaper”

1. **Cheaper than the seller's previous asking price.** This demonstrates a lower ask, not automatically a bargain.
2. **Cheaper than closely comparable registered sales.** This can support a valuation argument, subject to timing, size, floor, building, condition and property rights.
3. **Better value after all costs and risks.** This is the real investment test.

A useful buyer asks for evidence of all three; one does not replace the others.

## 01 | Is the slowdown real?

**The evidence:** Savills' Q3 analysis reports about 34,500 residential transactions, 4% fewer than Q2, with AED 70.8 billion in transaction value. The firm describes moderation in transaction activity and new launches, but says changes in the property mix help explain headline trends and like-for-like pricing has been relatively stable. [S1]

Another Q3 measure, attributed to Cavendish Maxwell, records **about 34,000 residential sales**, **38% lower year-on-year**, with value of **AED 72.6 billion**, down **47% year-on-year**. [S3] Those percentages use **Q3 2025** as the comparison, whereas the Savills 4% compares **Q3 2026 with Q2 2026**. The two conclusions are not contradictory. The volume/value totals are also not identical because providers use distinct samples and registration treatment.

**What the figures tell us:** Buyers are acting more carefully. Some property segments show fewer registrations. But transaction activity can respond to new-project launch schedules and registration lags, not only the willingness of sellers to lower prices.

**What they do not tell us:** These counts cannot establish an average negotiated discount on homes, nor can they prove that a particular community has become inexpensive.

**Client action:** When someone says “Dubai sales fell 38%,” ask: *Compared with when? Which types of transactions? And did similar homes actually reprice?*

## 02 | Have prices actually fallen?

**The evidence:** ValuStrat's September VPI measured a **4.5% annual decrease**, with apartments down **6.4%** and villas down **3.4%**. The monthly headline change was only **−0.1%**. [S2]

Savills, using a different approach, said Q3 headline values softened but like-for-like prices were relatively stable. [S1]

**Why can both be true?**

- A market index attempts to measure the trend in values across a set of properties.
- The median price per square foot is the middle transaction price in a sample. It changes when a different mix of homes sells.
- A particular two-bedroom apartment in a particular building has its *own* comparable sales, condition and negotiating circumstances.

A cheaper *citywide average* is not the same thing as a cheaper *specific home*. Equally, a resilient citywide median can hide weaker pockets.

**DPI interpretation:** We should not describe Dubai as being uniformly “on sale”. The more defensible statement is that repricing is selective and demands unit-level evidence.

**Client action:** Request the latest registered sales for similar units in the *same building or an appropriate comparable group*. Adjust transparently for floor, view, size, layout, age, condition and date.

## 03 | A five-percent discount can still be disappointing

This is a hypothetical teaching example, not an actual Dubai listing or a forecast.

**Apartment A — original proposition**

- Purchase price: **AED 1,000,000**
- Annual collected rent assumed: **AED 80,000**
- Annual operating costs assumed: **AED 15,000**
- Gross rental yield: **8.0%**
- Net operating income: **AED 65,000**
- Net operating yield, before finance and acquisition costs: **6.5%**

**The seller offers 5% off:** Purchase price becomes **AED 950,000**. If rent and operating expenses stay unchanged, net operating yield improves to approximately **6.84%**.

Sounds attractive. But what if the achievable annual rent turns out to be **AED 72,000**, not AED 80,000? At the discounted purchase price, after the same AED 15,000 operating expense assumption, the yield becomes **6.0%**. The discount did not rescue the original income expectation.

| Illustration | Price | Annual rent | Annual operating costs | Net operating yield |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Original assumptions | AED 1,000,000 | AED 80,000 | AED 15,000 | 6.50% |
| 5% price discount; rent holds | AED 950,000 | AED 80,000 | AED 15,000 | 6.84% |
| 5% discount; achievable rent 10% lower | AED 950,000 | AED 72,000 | AED 15,000 | 6.00% |

**Important:** Figures exclude transaction and registration fees, agent fees, any VAT where applicable, financing, insurance and other possible costs; they also assume expenses stay fixed. “Annual rent” means actual achievable collected rent, not a portal asking price.

**The lesson:** A discount is one number. Investment quality is the whole equation.

**Client action:** Verify achievable rent using current, comparable evidence. Check annual service charges against the official DLD/RERA Service Charge Index before accepting an advertised yield. [S5]

## 04 | Where should buyers investigate more carefully?

### Three community signals — not three bargains

The **frozen September 2026 edition** of Dubai Real Estate Data uses registered Dubai Land Department transactions and reports the change in median AED per square foot for **ready (resale) apartments and villas only**, comparing the **12 months ending 30 September 2026** with the **preceding 12 months**. Each area must have **at least 50 ready sales in each period** to appear in its ranked screen. Source data was captured by the provider as registered by 12 October; the edition was published on 15 October. [S8] [S9]

| Screening area | Ready-resale median, AED/sq ft | Year-on-year median change | Signal, not verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Dubai Science Park | 1,375 | −9.7% | Investigate comparable ready-apartment sales and condition |
| Al Furjan | 1,144 | −5.4% | Compare older and newer stock separately before judging |
| Al Kifaf | 1,947 | −5.3% | Check building, unit type and sample size carefully |

**Interpretation:** These communities deserve closer examination because *their ready-resale area medians* have moved lower in a comparable methodological screen. They are **not a ranking of investment returns, developer quality, affordability or available discounts**. Even ready-only medians can change when different buildings, sizes or grades trade. There are no matched-unit or matched-building sale pairs here, and the provider does not publish the exact prior-window counts in this summary. That limits the confidence of any claim about a specific property's true repricing.

**Confidence:** High that the September frozen edition reports these figures; **medium** that they indicate weaker area-level resale price momentum; **low / unassessed** that a specific listed home is now a bargain. The figures were not independently recomputed by DPI from raw DLD records.

### An illuminating contradiction: Al Furjan

An updated **19 October** snapshot from the *same data provider* shows **+14.2% year-on-year** in Al Furjan's median price per square foot for **all registered built-home sales** on its areas board, while its **ready-resale-only** momentum board shows **−7.0%**. [S10] The all-sales number is exposed to off-plan launch mix, whereas the ready resale screen excludes those launches. The precise dataset also advanced by several days after the frozen September edition, which is why the **−7.0% latest value must not be substituted into the frozen-table −5.4%** without also changing its reference period.

**DPI lesson:** Two true statistics about one community can tell different stories when they measure different kinds of property. That is not proof of manipulation, nor proof of widespread distress — it is a reason to read the definition first.

> The area is a starting point. The building, the unit, the rent and the exit decide whether it is an opportunity.

### Our recommended next research step — actual matched sales

For any property DPI might mention as a **genuine buyer opportunity**, collect at least several registered ready-home sales with the same:
- Building / immediate comparable catchment and property rights
- Apartment or villa type, bedrooms, approximate usable area and condition
- Close transaction dates, with any adjustments explained
- Distinction between actual registered prices and marketed asking prices

Compare actual negotiated offers against these matched transactions; then check service charges, achievable rent, financing and competing deliveries. **No building-level “verified discount” claim is approved for this draft.**

### How buyers could act — without treating these as recommendations

A buyer looking at Al Furjan can ask if the exact apartment has fallen below recently traded comparable ready homes — **not** whether the community's all-sales median is up or down. In Al Kifaf, a thinner screen particularly increases the need for property-level verification. In Dubai Science Park, compare new launches and existing homes separately, instead of assuming a broad price drop applies to both.

Off-plan incentives and gross rental yields belong to separate tests; they cannot be used as substitutes for a comparable sale price.

## 05 | Why future supply matters — but is not a crystal ball

Savills reports **more than 525,000 homes scheduled for delivery from 2027 to 2030**. [S1] This is a *scheduled pipeline*, not a confirmed number of future completed homes. Delays and revisions can materially alter the timing.

More competing units could give tenants or buyers more choices in some submarkets. But the effect on a particular property depends on nearby delivery schedules, unit mix, location, employment links, tenant demand and the condition of existing stock.

**DPI interpretation:** Delivery risk belongs in today's buying decision even if market-wide projections look comfortable.

**Client action:** For off-plan buyers, review escrow and project milestones where available, realistic handover dates, nearby competing launches and exit liquidity. For ready buyers, check new competing completions within the relevant catchment.

## DPI Buyer Opportunity Test

Before describing a property as a bargain, make sure the answer to each question is supported.

| Question | Evidence to request | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **1. What actually sold?** | Recent registered, closely matched transaction comparables | Asking prices can mislead |
| **2. What will it earn?** | Achievable rental comparables, vacancy, operating costs | Gross yield is not net return |
| **3. What is the full price?** | Property price plus applicable fees, financing and fit-out | Cash needed can be much higher |
| **4. What could go wrong?** | Building condition, supply pipeline, developer schedule and legal checks | Risks can outweigh apparent savings |
| **5. Who buys it later?** | Resale volume, buyer demand, likely holding period | Entry price matters only alongside an exit |

### Three possible verdicts

- **Evidence-backed opportunity:** Competitive against genuine comparable sales **and** sustainable on conservative income and risk assumptions.
- **Watchlist opportunity:** Price looks interesting, but at least one material input is missing or uncertain.
- **Illusion of opportunity:** The “discount” exists only against an inflated asking price, or costs and risks destroy the expected return.

These are editorial labels, **not automated DPI asset ratings or investment advice**.

---

## What to tell clients this week

> “I wouldn't buy because the headline says prices are falling. I would buy only after we check recent sales for similar properties, what it will realistically cost and earn, and how easily it could be sold later.”

**For first-time buyers:** Compare the full cash needed with the cost of continuing to rent, including maintenance and finance.  
**For income investors:** Test net income under a lower-rent or temporary-vacancy scenario.  
**For off-plan investors:** Treat handover timing, future competing supply and payment obligations as core underwriting inputs, not footnotes.  
**For advisors:** Present the data scope, confidence and three concrete reasons for the recommendation.

## DPI's conclusion

The right question is not **“Has Dubai become cheaper?”**

It is **“Is this particular property now worth buying, on evidence we can explain?”**

The market's transition may create opportunities. It may also create persuasive discount marketing. A strong investor learns to separate the two.

**Know Today. Invest Better. Build Tomorrow.**

## Editorial methodology and limitations

1. This draft was researched on 25 October 2026. Cite publication dates and price-index reference periods separately.
2. Savills and ValuStrat figures are **provider-reported**; DPI has not rebuilt their underlying transaction-level dataset. Do not label the derived provider figures “DLD verified by DPI.”
3. Savills (Q3 2026 ~34,500, AED 70.8B) and Cavendish Maxwell (Q3 2026 ~34,000, AED 72.6B) have **different scope/processing**. Their totals must **never be added or averaged**. Also, the former's −4% QoQ and the latter's −38% YoY have different baseline periods.
4. Issue 003 included Property Monitor's September 10,373 residential transactions; later DLD aggregations from other outlets may count more sales because of different inclusions and refresh dates. Do not silently revise Issue 003 or treat the scopes as identical.
5. Price *indices*, median *prices*, asking *prices* and negotiated discounts are distinct measurements.
6. Recent DLD registrations are subject to lag and revisions. DLD open-data explorer requires interactive filters/verification; direct raw data extraction has not been completed for this draft.
7. For supply, “scheduled” is not “delivered.” For index movements, “value index” is not a price discount that applies to every unit.
8. All teaching-example inputs are **hypothetical** and exclude finance and acquisition costs; no property-level claim is being made.
9. Three area-level resale momentum screens have been added (Section 04), with identical frozen-edition definitions. They are not matched-unit comparables. Do not upgrade them to claims of investment bargains without obtaining building- and unit-level matched registrations.
10. This publication is independent educational research, not personalized financial, investment, legal or tax advice.
