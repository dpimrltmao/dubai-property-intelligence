PAGE 02 — The central message

Demand is not only in Al Barsha. Dubai is not one market.

Some districts may record more new leases; others fewer. Neither observation by itself tells us how many new households need a home, whether supply is being absorbed, or whether owners can raise rents.

This issue examines three selected DLD administrative areas—Madinat Al Mataar (462), Wadi Al Safa 5 (467) and Al Barsha South Fourth (441). We chose them because their measures diverge. They are not Dubai's only rental-demand centres, not a representative citywide sample and not ranked investment opportunities.

The three indicators must stay separate:

  1. Rental activity: How many eligible single-property New rental contracts have start dates in our comparison period? A newly starting contract can reflect a tenant relocating within Dubai rather than a new household.
  2. Rental pricing: What is the median annual rent for the selected one-bedroom cohort? A change can reflect bargaining pressure or a shift in the mix, size or condition of leased units.
  3. Scheduled development: How many ACTIVE project records carry a 2027 target end date, and what progress do those records report? A target is not a verified residential handover.

Editor's verdict: Our data supports a story of uneven market indicators and local rental-pricing conditions, not a claim that only one area has demand or that Dubai is facing a demonstrated citywide oversupply.

Method note: DLD rent and transaction snapshots dated 20 November 2026; project snapshot dated 1 November 2026. Descriptive observations, not measured net absorption.

PAGE 04 — The 2027 project pipeline

A target date is not a handover.

There are 362 ACTIVE project records in the 1 November 2026 DLD extract with a recorded end date in 2027.

Of those, 303 (83.7%) are recorded below 50% construction progress, 44 (12.2%) between 50% and under 75%, and 15 (4.1%) at least 75%.

This is an unweighted comparison of project records. It is not the percentage of Dubai's 2027 homes that will be late, not a residential unit-delivery count, and not a prediction that any specific developer will miss its target.

What to investigate: Verify updated completion status, intended residential use, actual handovers and directly competing housing stock before treating a project's reported date as future available supply.

PAGE 08 — A stricter rental sensitivity test

Does the softer-rent direction survive grouping by recorded project name?

For eligible New one-bedroom apartment leases in Al Barsha South Fourth, we used DLD field project_name_en, restricted apartment sizes to 35–125 m² and retained name groups with at least ten qualifying leases in each Jan–Oct year.

127 recorded project-name groups passed the threshold:

  • 108 recorded a lower median annual rent in 2026;
  • 11 were unchanged;
  • 8 recorded a higher median.

Interpretation: The observed lower-rent direction appears across many recurring project-name groups, not just in the area-wide median.

Necessary caveat: A project name can represent several buildings. Sizes, floors, furnishing, conditions and tenant mix are not held constant. This is not a matched-building or same-unit rent index. It cannot establish causality or actual net absorption.

PAGE 09 — Three plausible outcomes, not three forecasts

What evidence would change the verdict?

A · Healthy absorption: Verified completed housing reaches tenants, while comparable rents and occupancy remain resilient. Requires evidence of actual completions and occupancy, not merely new contracts.

B · Geographically uneven pressure: Some comparable completed properties soften while others remain firm because tenant preferences, price brackets, location and handover timing differ.

C · Pressure in specific segments: Comparable rents and independently measured occupancy weaken in the same places and property types where completed, marketable supply increases.

The decision checklist for a buyer or landlord

  • What rent do comparable occupied or recently leased homes achieve—not just advertised rents?
  • What vacancy allowance, service charge, maintenance and financing costs fit the downside case?
  • Which competing buildings are already delivered, and which are only forecast?
  • Who is the likely next buyer, and what would a credible exit price require?

DPI verdict for November: We have enough verified evidence to reject a simplistic "one Dubai market" narrative. We do not yet have enough to calculate citywide household absorption, certify specific 2027 handovers or rank Dubai's best investment areas.

PAGE 10 — Sources, filters and boundaries

Transparency is part of the result.

Data origin and period: Publisher-supplied DLD transaction and rental CSV exports dated 20 November 2026, projects CSV dated 1 November 2026. All paired comparisons are 1 January–31 October 2025 versus 1 January–31 October 2026.

Rental sample: Residential Flat/Studio or Villa/Complex Villas, one property and first contract line, standard lease length 300–430 days, plausible annual rental amount AED 5,000–2,000,000; distinct eligible contract IDs. The 989,471 figure includes both New and Renew. Area New-start counts use the New subset; one-bedroom medians use a further apartment filter. Date is contract start, not necessarily DLD registration or move-in.

Sales sample: Residential apartments and villas with accepted transaction procedure, value, size and implied price per square foot. The 261,410 figure covers qualifying sale registrations in the paired periods; the area dossiers' narrower metric is Existing Properties + procedure Sell for apartments. This is not certified resale-only, and changes in medians are not mix-adjusted appreciation.

Projects: DLD area identifiers, recorded ACTIVE status, percent_completed and project_end_date are registry fields. Project counts are not completed dwellings or occupancy. The January–October comparisons should not be mixed with a November project snapshot as if they described the same interval.

Areas: DLD area 462 Madinat Al Mataar, 467 Wadi Al Safa 5, 441 Al Barsha South Fourth. These three are selected contrasting case studies, not comprehensive Dubai coverage or area recommendations.

We cannot measure from these extracts alone: net new resident households, population-to-housing conversion, occupied or vacant home totals, true community absorption rates, validated future handovers, guaranteed prices or investment returns. Registration lag, transaction mix, project-name overlap and rent-unit differences limit inference.

Data portal: https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/open-data/real-estate-data/
