## Editorial purpose
Issue 004 showed how a discount could be an illusion. Issue 005 tests whether 2027 housing demand is sufficiently strong, *and in which micro-markets*, to absorb delivered homes. This is neither a blanket oversupply warning nor a prediction of a city-wide price crash.

**Central research question:** When Dubai adds housing stock, who will occupy or purchase that stock, at what affordable price, and where is the gap most visible?

## Three testable hypotheses (do not decide before evidence)
A. **Broad absorption:** Population/household growth, leasing and end-user demand absorb incremental deliveries.
B. **Uneven absorption:** Competition and rental concessions increase in certain catchments/unit types while established locations hold.
C. **Demand shortage:** Completed supply significantly outpaces verified occupancy/leasing and resale liquidity, generating sustained local correction.

Evidence required to choose among hypotheses: *actual* completions, comparable registered resale, new rentals vs renewals, rent levels and vacancy/occupation proxies where available, affordability/financing and catchment-level concentration. Absence of direct vacancy data must be acknowledged; rental registrations are not equivalent to net household formation.

## Non-negotiable data definitions
1. **Stock:** approved/registered projects vs under-construction vs estimated handovers vs verified completed/occupied units. Never equate scheduled units with delivered keys.
2. **Demand:** existing tenant movements vs genuinely new lease uptake, household formation, migration/population, homebuyer registrations, vacancy proxy limits. New rental contract registrations are not a direct absorption rate.
3. **Prices:** asking vs registered; median vs mix-adjusted index; off-plan vs ready; apartment vs villa; area-level vs building comparables.
4. **Geography:** choose 3-4 catchments *from evidence*, not anecdotal favorites. Compare comparable unit mix and delivery timing.
5. **Timing:** show baseline and source observation date; settled quarterly data preferred to incomplete trailing month.
6. **Mortgage:** floating benchmark EIBOR is not the full bank offer rate; all amortization illustrations labeled hypothetical.
